WEEK 4 - SIEM IN PRACTICE
07. CORRELATION AND TUNING

1. What is event correlation?
Event correlation is the process of connecting multiple events to identify a meaningful pattern. A single event may be harmless, but a sequence of related events can indicate suspicious behavior.

Example:
Several failed logins -> a successful login from the same source -> unusual privileged activity.
When these events share a user, host, source, and timeline, they may form a stronger detection than any one event alone.

2. Correlation rules
A correlation rule combines conditions across events or data sources.
Example:
If five failed logins for the same account occur from one source IP within five minutes, create an alert. Increase priority if a successful login follows shortly afterward.
Use correlation when the relationship between events is important, not just the presence of one event.

3. Thresholds
A threshold is a numeric limit that triggers an action when a count or value reaches it.
Example:
Alert when failed_logins >= 5 in five minutes.
Use thresholds for repeated or unusually frequent activity. Set the value using observed behavior, risk, and testing; there is no universal threshold that fits every organization.

4. Baselines
A baseline describes normal activity for a system, user, network, or time period.
Example:
A server normally receives a small number of SSH login failures per day. A sudden increase at an unusual time may be suspicious.
Use baselines to identify deviations from normal behavior. Account for shifts, maintenance windows, seasonality, and changes in business activity.

5. Allowlists
An allowlist is a narrowly defined set of known, approved entities or activities that should be treated differently by a detection.
Example:
A verified vulnerability scanner may be allowlisted for a specific scan schedule and target range.
Use allowlists only when ownership, purpose, scope, and expiry/review are documented. An allowlist should not broadly exclude an entire internal network or all activity from a trusted-looking address.

6. Comparison

Method | What it does | Best used when | Main risk
Correlation rule | Connects related events or conditions | A sequence or relationship indicates risk | Incorrect joins or time windows can create noise or miss events
Threshold | Triggers after a count/value reaches a limit | Repeated activity or volume matters | Too high misses slow activity; too low creates false positives
Baseline | Compares current activity with normal behavior | Behavior varies by user, host, or time | Poor or outdated baseline creates bad decisions
Allowlist | Excludes or treats approved activity differently | A known source or activity is verified and scoped | Overbroad or stale exclusions hide attacks

7. How to tune a detection
1) Review a sample of alerts and the raw events.
2) Identify common false-positive causes.
3) Confirm the data fields and timestamps are correct.
4) Check whether the threshold and time window fit normal activity.
5) Add context such as asset, account, source, and business schedule.
6) Make the smallest safe change.
7) Test the detection against known benign and suspicious examples.
8) Document the reason, owner, scope, and review date.
9) Monitor false positives and false negatives after the change.

8. Example: tuning a brute-force alert
Initial logic: five failed logins in five minutes.
Observed issue: a known scanner creates repeated failures during an approved test.
Safe tuning:
- Verify the scanner owner and approved source address.
- Restrict the exception to the scanner's exact source, target systems, and approved time window.
- Keep monitoring other sources and unexpected activity.
- Set a review/expiry date for the exception.
- Continue alerting if a successful login or unusual follow-on activity occurs, where supported.

9. Important principles
- Do not tune only to reduce alert count.
- Preserve visibility for high-risk assets and privileged accounts.
- Prefer narrow, documented exceptions.
- Re-test after rule or parser changes.
- Keep an audit trail of detection changes.
- Revisit baselines as the environment changes.

CONCLUSION
Correlation connects events, thresholds detect repeated activity, baselines identify deviations, and allowlists handle verified approved activity. Effective tuning reduces noise while protecting detection coverage.
